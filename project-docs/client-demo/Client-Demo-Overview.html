How it works, end to end

Eight Steps From Raw Data to an Approved Number.

Every run follows the same path. The first six steps run automatically in Databricks, in about ten minutes for the current data. The last two involve people: reviewing what the checks found, and approving the fixes.

01

Ingest

Data arrives from every source: file drops today, databases and application APIs as connectors are added. Each record is tagged with its source, country and run.

02

Standardise

Dates, amounts, currency codes and field names are put into one standard format, whatever system they came from.

03

Validate and clean

More than 25 checks: missing values, invalid codes, impossible amounts, broken links. Failing records are held back with the reason.

04

Detect

Transactions are screened by 9 rules: large amounts, structuring, unusual activity, duplicates and more.

05

Aggregate

KPIs, portfolio, branch, country and scenario figures are calculated from the verified data only.

06

Reconcile

What each source sent is compared with what was kept, and the Bank's data with its core banking system. Every gap is recorded.

07

Review and approve

Each exception becomes a task for the right team. Large decisions need a second approver, and the CFO signs off.

08

Correct and refresh

Approved corrections are saved immediately and applied to the data on the next run. The dashboards update.

Steps 4 and 5 · Detect and aggregate

Unusual Activity Flagged. The Bank's KPIs Calculated.

Once the data is clean, two things happen in parallel: every transaction is screened for unusual patterns, and the figures the board and regulators watch are calculated. Every number is computed from the verified data, never typed in.

Transaction screening: three kinds of flag

Suspicious

Patterns that may point to fraud or money laundering: structuring just under a limit, money in and out the same day, a dormant account suddenly active.

Goes to: investigations

Threshold

A reporting event, not fraud: for example a single transaction above 50,000 USD.

Goes to: compliance

Operational

A processing fault, such as the same transaction posted twice.

Goes to: operations

Grouped into cases, not a flood of alerts. Related flags on the same account and day become one case with a severity. High and medium cases become tasks with a deadline; low ones go into a daily digest.

Also calculated: loan book by product, segment, branch and currency · IFRS 9 staging · top 20 exposures · ageing · loan-to-value bands · branch, segment and country performance · the snapshot behind stress testing.

Limits and breaches. Each KPI is compared with its limit at three levels: early warning, internal appetite and regulatory minimum. A breach becomes a compliance task with a deadline.

* Uses a documented assumption until the Bank supplies the missing inputs (deposit rates, cost allocation, equity). Shown on screen with a footnote.

What changes for the Bank

Fraud is not "a big number"

A large transaction is labelled a reporting event, not fraud. Each flag says why it was raised, so investigators start with a reason.

KPIs ready every morning

No one builds the ratios by hand. They are recalculated every run, on the same definitions, for every country.

Converted at a logged rate

Figures in LBP, SAR, QAR and EUR are converted to USD, and every exchange rate used is recorded with its time.

Step 6 · Reconcile

Hundreds of Differences. A Handful of Decisions.

Reconciliation answers two different questions, and the platform asks both on every run. Then, instead of sending people through differences one by one, it clears the harmless ones, groups the rest by cause, and asks for one decision per group.

Safety rules

Big items are never bulk-cleared

Missing records, key fields and differences of 10,000 or more always get their own task, due in one day.

Two people for large totals

A group totalling 100,000 or more needs a second approver. The first decision-maker can't give it.

Nothing is re-accepted quietly

A difference that was decided and comes back in a later run is reopened as "recurring", and the day's run is signed off by a second person.

Steps 7 and 8 · Review, approve, correct

Every Exception Owned. Every Fix Approved.

A finding is only useful if someone acts on it. Every exception the platform finds becomes a task with an owner, a priority and a deadline, decided in one place, with a permanent record of who did what.

The CFO approval loop for a reconciliation gap

1 · Gap found

The run finds rows rejected or amounts that moved. One item is created for the CFO.

2 · CFO reviews

The CFO opens the rejected records, then handles the item directly or assigns it to a colleague.

3 · Values corrected

The assignee proposes the corrected values with a comment, and submits back.

4 · CFO approves

The CFO approves, or returns it with a note. The loop repeats until it is right.

5 · Numbers update

Saved immediately, applied in the next run, so the gap closes and every KPI includes the fixed record.

Built to scale

Ten Sources Today. A Hundred Tomorrow.

The processing runs on Databricks, which spreads work across as many machines as the data needs. Adding sources, countries or years of history does not require a new platform, only more computing power, which can be increased at any time and scaled back when the run is done.

01More sources

Each source is a configuration, not a rebuild.

A new country or system is one more source: a connection plus a mapping to the standard format. Checks, reconciliation, tasks and screens then apply automatically.

02More data

More volume, more compute. Not more people.

Millions of transactions are handled by adding workers to the run. Heavier runs cost more compute; the steps and the results stay the same.

03Same speed on screen

The screens never touch the raw data.

Each run pre-calculates small summary tables, and the screens read only those. The design target is under two seconds per screen, whatever the volume underneath.

What grows easily
  • Number of sources and countries: 10, 20, 50 or 100
  • Data volume: from thousands to millions of rows a night, by adding compute
  • New checks and rules: thresholds and limits are settings, changed without code
  • New reports and screens: built on the same verified data
What each new source still needs
  • A connector: file drop, database or API, depending on how the system can share data
  • A mapping of its fields and transaction codes to the standard format, confirmed by the Bank
  • Agreed record keys, so the same customer or account is recognised across systems
  • Compute budget: larger runs cost more; the Bank chooses the speed/cost balance
